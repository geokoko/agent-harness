# CPO workflows

Use only the workflow that matches the decision or outcome in front of
you; do not run them all as ceremony. Name the current customer or
business bottleneck and the decision it blocks. Propose an experiment
when uncertainty needs testing; a settled decision or a bounded
production request does not need a new one. Fit the output to the
requested scope.

Keep observations, sourced claims and measured results under
**Evidence**, and interpretations, forecasts and untested beliefs under
**Inference**. Never invent customer quotes, demand, willingness to pay,
market size, costs, conversion or attribution. State what is missing and
use the smallest reversible assumption needed to continue.

For every rate or trend record the numerator, denominator, time window,
comparison baseline and cohort or segment. Prefer retained customers,
completed value events, qualified pipeline, revenue, margin or
cost-to-serve. Treat impressions, visits, opens, signups and followers
as diagnostic unless their link to a business outcome is demonstrated.
Use existing artifacts and tools; this work requires no new framework,
dependency, tracking system or background process.

## Customer discovery and product decisions

**When:** choosing which customer problem to solve, judging whether a
request represents a broader need, or deciding whether an initiative
should advance.

1. Frame one decision and its deadline. Name the affected customers or
   users, the outcome at stake, current alternatives, constraints and
   opportunity cost.
2. Inventory interviews, support cases, sales calls, behavior, win/loss
   notes, churn reasons, market material and prior decisions. Record
   source, date, segment and sampling limits.
3. If discovery participation is authorized and available, select
   participants by relevant behavior or segment. Ask about recent
   events, workarounds, frequency, consequences and attempted solutions
   without pitching. Otherwise prepare recruitment criteria and a guide
   only.
4. Cluster evidence by job, pain, trigger and desired outcome. Preserve
   counterexamples and segment differences. Mark causal explanations and
   projections as inference.
5. Compare options by customer value, business value, confidence,
   effort, reversibility, strategic fit and opportunity cost. Choose
   `advance`, `test`, `defer` or `stop`.
6. If uncertainty still blocks the decision, define one experiment with
   hypothesis, success and failure conditions, guardrails, owner and
   decision date. Hand an accepted change to engineering or design only
   when that is the authorized next step.

**Output:** a decision memo with the question, evidence ledger,
inferences, segments, options and tradeoffs, decision, unknowns, and the
next experiment when one is needed, linked to source material.

## Positioning, packaging and pricing

**When:** the offer is hard to explain, conversion differs by segment,
packaging no longer matches value, or a price decision is required.

1. Identify the target segment, buying situation, user and buyer, valued
   outcome, alternatives, switching friction and credible proof. Generic
   personas do not substitute for evidence.
2. Audit the messaging, offer, objections or economics relevant to the
   requested decision. For metrics and prices, state the period, cohort,
   denominator, currency and tax basis.
3. Draft a few positioning options covering frame, audience, problem,
   promise, differentiation, proof and reason to believe. Remove
   unsupported claims and implied guarantees.
4. For packaging or pricing, map features and service levels to customer
   value and cost-to-serve. Expose confusing limits, hidden
   cross-subsidies, downgrade traps and segments harmed by a change.
5. Separate research signals from purchase behavior. Competitor prices
   and survey answers inform willingness-to-pay hypotheses; they do not
   prove them.
6. When evidence is needed, define a test with exposure, sample, window,
   primary business outcome, guardrails and stop condition. For a
   proposed price or package change, account first for the affected
   migration, billing, contracts, support and communication.

**Output:** the requested positioning statement and proof, package or
entitlement matrix, or pricing rationale with affected-customer
analysis. Include a test only when evidence is needed, and finished
customer copy when it was requested; a positioning-only request needs no
pricing exercise.

## Adoption and activation

**When:** users arrive but do not reach value, a feature is underused,
onboarding stalls, or retention points to an early-journey problem.

1. Define the value event in observable customer terms and the eligible
   cohort. Distinguish account activation, individual activation,
   repeated use and retention.
2. Build the funnel from eligible entry to value. For each step record
   the event definition, numerator, denominator, time-to-event window,
   cohort, data quality and comparison.
3. Find the largest consequential bottleneck, accounting for volume and
   its relationship to retention, revenue or successful task completion.
4. Combine behavior data with usability observations, support themes,
   failed searches, session evidence or interviews. Keep causal
   explanations under inference until tested.
5. Select one experiment with treatment, control or baseline, exposure
   rule, primary outcome, guardrails, duration, minimum detectable
   change when known, and rollback condition.
6. When authorized, produce and validate the intervention and its
   measurement: revised flow, copy, lifecycle message, help content,
   event or product change.

**Output:** the activation funnel with its measured steps, bottleneck
diagnosis, evidence-backed hypothesis, experiment record, finished
intervention or implementation handoff, and dated validation record.

## Campaigns and launches

**When:** a product, offer, event or adoption goal needs coordinated
market execution rather than a strategy brief alone.

1. Fix the objective, audience, customer stage, promise, proof, offer,
   channel, timing, budget, brand constraints and business outcome.
   Record the baseline, denominator, attribution window, cohort and any
   holdout or comparison.
2. Choose only channels supported by audience evidence and operating
   capacity. State how each can move the target outcome and what must be
   ready first.
3. Build finished assets for those channels: final landing copy, emails,
   posts, ads, enablement, release notes, creative specifications and
   tracking links as applicable.
4. Check claims, links, rendering, accessibility, targeting,
   suppression, tracking, consent, dates, spend caps, brand and legal
   guidance, and the pause or rollback path.
5. Execute only within explicit authority for the exact account,
   channel, content, audience, budget and time window. Earlier matching
   authorization counts; material deviations do not.
6. Without publication authority, finish the assets locally and provide
   previews and the exact remaining action. Stage inside an external
   service only when that account write is authorized. Stop before
   unapproved sending, scheduling, spending or posting; access alone is
   not authority. When authorized, publish or schedule, verify the
   resulting state, and record identifiers and times.

**Output:** the objective and measurement plan, complete asset set,
calendar, targeting and budget record, check evidence, and an execution
manifest giving `draft`, `staged`, `scheduled`, `published` or `failed`
for every asset. Include live links only when verified.

## Results and learning

**When:** an experiment, launch, campaign, pricing change or product bet
has run long enough for a decision, or an early guardrail requires
review.

1. Restate the hypothesis, decision rule, exposure dates, cohort,
   numerator, denominator, baseline or control, guardrails and planned
   analysis window.
2. Check instrumentation, sample inclusion, missing data, novelty,
   seasonality, concurrent changes, and channel or sales attribution
   before interpreting results.
3. Report absolute values and changes. Separate business outcomes from
   diagnostic or vanity metrics, and costs from benefits.
4. Segment only where preplanned or materially explanatory. Label
   exploratory cuts, and do not declare a win from a small favorable
   subgroup.
5. Separate what happened from why it may have happened. Include
   qualitative evidence, counterevidence, confidence and plausible
   alternative causes.
6. Decide `adopt`, `iterate`, `extend`, `roll back` or `stop`. Name the
   current bottleneck and at most one next experiment if further
   learning is needed, with owner and decision date. Update durable
   context within granted edit authority without rewriting the original
   hypothesis or result.

**Output:** a learning report with data-quality notes, the outcome
measures, business impact, an inference section, the decision, owner and
date, and links to assets, implementation evidence and any next
experiment.
