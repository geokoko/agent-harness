# Personal exam-note preferences

Use practical Greek with English technical terms when appropriate to the
course, unless the user requests another language. Preserve all requested
coverage and wording when asked for complete or verbatim material; exam
frequency determines emphasis, never permission to omit required content.

Organize revision around recognizable exercise types: recognition cue,
applicable formula, assumptions, substitution, result, and common trap. Worked
numerical examples use **general formula → numerical substitution → result
with units**. Distinguish a definition or theorem statement from its proof;
provide a reusable proof pattern when the method recurs. Read the
[topic template](references/note-template.md) only when creating topic notes.

Classify supplied material by content as lecture notes, exams, or mixed material;
extraction quality is a separate assessment. Poor OCR does not make a lecture an
exam. Do not invent exam weighting when no past papers or weighting were supplied.

Keep source and page anchors near disputed formulas, diagrams, and claims.
Preserve symbol meanings, units, validity conditions, and complete requested
examples. Extracted PDF text can lose equations or silently corrupt symbols;
inspect rendered source pages where extraction is doubtful. Mark illegible or
conflicting material instead of guessing. Separate observed past-paper
recurrence from predictions.

Deliver only useful requested artifacts, with coverage gaps explicit. When
producing a PDF, inspect its rendered pages before claiming layout verification.
A source inventory, formula sheet, or question bank is optional, not a mandatory
package for every notes request.
